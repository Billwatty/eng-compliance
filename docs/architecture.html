Quality control

How quality is measured, judged and reported

Payment checks only work if the quality checks under them are right. This section covers what each test is measured against, where those limits come from, when each piece of data enters the system, how a deviation is calculated, and which reports come out.

1. What the work is measured against

The contract decides. When two documents give different limits, the contract's priority-of-documents clause says which one wins. The usual order on a Ghana road contract is:

  1. Particular or special specification. Changes written for this contract.
  2. Standard Specification for Road and Bridge Works. The Ghana Highway Authority and Ministry of Roads and Highways specification that sets most limits for earthworks, pavement layers, asphalt, concrete and bridges.
  3. Referenced product and test standards. GS 788-2:2018 for reinforcing steel, which is close to BS 4449. Also the cement standard, and the BS EN, AASHTO or GHA test methods named in the specification.
  4. Approved project values. These are the job mix formula for asphalt, the concrete mix design, and the maximum dry density (MDD) of each approved gravel source. Many limits are written relative to these, such as "97% of MDD", so they are data the tool must store, not fixed constants.

2. Where the standard data comes from and how it gets in

  • Specification text: the consultant uploads the contract's specification PDFs at setup.
  • Draft rules: AI reads each clause and drafts a rule card with the clause reference. The materials engineer checks every card against the page, corrects it and approves it. Nothing is used until it is approved.
  • Paid standards: these go through the licence-gated standards library. When a publisher's AI licence is signed, AI extracts the limits for the engineer to approve. Until then, the engineer types the values from the firm's licensed copy, and only the numbers and clause numbers are stored.
  • Reference values: MDD, job mix formula and mix design come from approved lab tests and approval letters. Each rule that depends on one is linked to it.
  • Changes: an engineer's instruction or a variation creates a new rule version with an effective date. Every test is judged by the rule version in force on the day it was taken.

5. How deviation is measured

  • Against a minimum or maximum: deviation is the result minus the limit. A density of 96.8% against 97% is −0.2 points and fails.
  • Lot average: the mean of all tests in the lot, compared with the average limit.
  • Tolerance band: measured minus design. A base 22 mm thinner than design is inside +0 / −25 mm.
  • Envelope: each sieve is checked against its band, and the report names which sieves fall outside and by how much.
  • Statistical: concrete uses the mean of a group of cubes and the lowest single cube, compared with the class strength.
  • Test frequency: tests required minus tests done for the lot's area or volume. A shortfall is a failure even if every test passed.
  • Near-limit flag: results within a set margin of the limit are marked marginal. A run of results that all sit just above the limit is flagged for a witnessed retest.

Worked example. A lot of base course between chainage 12+000 and 12+500 is 7 m wide, so 3,500 m², which needs 10 density tests. The contractor submits 8.

6. Who tests, and why it matters

  • Contractor quality control: the contractor's own lab tests every lot. These results show the contractor's control of the work.
  • Consultant verification: the consultant witnesses or repeats a share of tests. The tool tracks that share per material and flags any material that falls below the plan.
  • Independent checks: the GHA Central Materials Laboratory or the auditors test on request. Large gaps between contractor and independent results are flagged.

Materials chain

From requirement to purchase, store, use and claim

Quality tests sample the finished work. They do not show whether the right material was bought, stored properly and used in the right amount. This module follows each material from the specification to the payment claim. It catches wrong grades, unapproved substitutes, cement that is short in the concrete, and payment for materials on site that are not actually in the store.

Checking use against design

The expected use is the installed quantity times the design rate. The design rate comes from the mix design, the bar bending schedule or the job mix formula. The variance is what the store issued minus the expected use.

  • Issued well above expected points to waste or loss. Contractors care most about this.
  • Issued below expected points to under-dosing, which is a quality risk. It triggers extra testing on the affected pours.

Who uses this part

  • The contractor's storekeeper and quality manager become daily users. Stock loss control is something a contractor may pay for on its own. This is a hypothesis to test in interviews.
  • The consultant sees grades, quantities, certificates and match results. Purchase prices stay in the contractor's view only, because contractors will not share them.

Ideas taken from AI quality management, and ideas left out

Included

  • Corrective and preventive action linked to each NCR, with root causes tracked across NCRs
  • Supplier scorecards built from rejected deliveries, late certificates and failed tests
  • Early warnings from near-limit trends and consumption variance
  • Audit-ready reports generated from the ledger

Left out, and why

  • Scraping standards from the web: standards come only from licensed copies, through the licence-gated standards library.
  • Zero data entry: Ghana sites run on paper, so fast photo and WhatsApp capture is the real job.
  • A CAD design copilot: roads are designed before the contract, by others. Our users build and supervise.
  • AI that stops payments by itself: people sign. The tool holds a line for review and never certifies or blocks one alone.
  • Checking batch numbers against manufacturer databases: few exist for Ghana supply. The tool matches the mill certificate's heat number to a photo of the bar tag instead.

Standards library · in the MVP

Using ISO, ASTM, BSI and GSA standards under licence

The MVP includes a full standards library. AI reads a standard, finds the acceptance limits, and drafts rule cards that cite the exact clause. Every standard passes a licence gate first, so a publisher's content is processed only for the uses its licence allows. Licences will be negotiated once the proposal is accepted. Until a licence is signed, that publisher's standards use manual limit entry, and the rest of the product works the same.

What the MVP builds

  • Licence register: one record per publisher, listing the licence holder, permitted uses (view, AI extraction, storing excerpts), the customers covered and the expiry date.
  • Licence gate: checked on every upload and every extraction. Content without an AI licence is refused for AI processing and routed to manual entry.
  • Clause index: encrypted, separated per customer, and never used to train any model. Excerpts shown to users are limited to the clause being cited.
  • Limit extraction: AI drafts the limit, units, test method and clause reference. An engineer approves each one before it enters the rule library.
  • Usage log: records which standard, clause and customer each extraction served. It feeds the usage reports publishers usually require.

What to ask for when negotiating

  1. AI-assisted extraction of requirements from standards the customer has licensed, for that customer's named users only.
  2. No model training, no sharing between customers, and content deleted when the licence ends.
  3. Outputs that show the clause number and a short excerpt, linking back to the publisher's own copy.
  4. Usage reports each quarter, with pricing either as an uplift per user or per project, or as a revenue share.

Suggested order: GSA first, because it has the strongest local interest and the fastest path. Then ISO through GSA. Then BSI, because Ghana specifications reference many BS and BS EN test methods. ASTM comes last, and only where a contract cites ASTM methods.

Questions answered

What does AI do, and what never uses AI?

  • AI reads and proposes. It extracts fields from scans and photos, drafts rules from specification text, and suggests which BOQ item and chainage a document belongs to.
  • Rules decide pass or fail. Every check is a rule an engineer approved, so the same inputs always give the same result.
  • People accept. Inspectors and quantity surveyors clear exceptions, and the Resident Engineer signs. The tool never certifies anything.

How does it work with poor signal?

  • The Android app saves every record on the phone first, with time and GPS stamped on the device.
  • A sync queue uploads records when signal returns. Photos are compressed before upload.
  • WhatsApp is the fallback for contractors and labs who will not install another app.

How do we stop test results being changed?

  • Every file is hashed when it arrives and stored write-once. An edit creates a new version, and the old one stays.
  • Labs enter results from their own accounts, against a tag ID created at sampling time.
  • Long gaps between sampling and testing are flagged, and so are results that sit suspiciously close to the limit every time.

What happens when documents disagree?

  • Conflicting revisions: every rule carries its source, revision and date. The contract's priority-of-documents clause decides between them. If no approved precedence rule covers the case, the tool shows the conflict and does not pick.
  • Substitutions: a different product passes only with a linked approval from the engineer.
  • Missing evidence: the item shows as missing and is held back. The tool never infers evidence.
  • Ambiguous matches: anything below the confidence threshold goes to review, with both documents shown side by side.

Who sees what, and where is data stored?

  • Each contract is its own workspace. Contractors see only their own submissions. The client and the World Bank get read-only views.
  • Ghana's Data Protection Act, 2012 (Act 843) applies. Registration with the Data Protection Commission needs to be confirmed. verify
  • Ask the client whether government contract data must stay in Ghana before choosing a hosting region. verify

What does it connect to?

  • For the pilot, nothing. Excel and PDF go in, and the IPC evidence pack comes out in the consultant's existing format.
  • Later: lab systems, World Bank reporting templates, and read-only payment dates from government finance systems to measure time to payment.

How do we prove it works?

  • Replay a completed contract month by month. Compare the tool's flags with items the Resident Engineer, the client, the World Bank or auditors later queried or rejected.
  • Track four numbers: share of known problems caught, false flags per IPC, review minutes per IPC, and days from claim to certificate.

What is it built with?

  • An offline-first Android app, plus intake through the WhatsApp Business Platform and email.
  • Postgres with PostGIS for chainage and GPS, and object storage with write-once locking for originals.
  • A job queue runs OCR and extraction with Claude models using structured output. Rules are versioned files that are reviewed like code.
